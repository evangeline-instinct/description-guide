Decoded upload guide

15 October 2026

Use the episode summary to write clear, accurate show notes, then prepare the right audio and release details.

Start here

For episodes I host, I'll share a Google Docs summary. Use that to draft the description, check it against the recording, and then prepare the episode in Spotify for Creators. The summary is the source for what we discussed; old descriptions are only style examples.

What every description needs

  1. The full names of the host and any cohosts who actually appear in that episode. Do not add Amaira or anyone else automatically.
  2. The guest's full name and a short introduction with their relevant achievements: role, institution, books, work or background. Use only facts supplied or checked for this episode.
  3. A few real talking points. Be specific about the questions, ideas, examples or stories we discussed, rather than just listing broad subjects such as AI or economics.
  4. Any confirmed research, outreach and audio credits, plus partnership or part-one/part-two context when relevant.

The recent interview descriptions use connected paragraphs: introduction, the main ideas, then a closing question or takeaway. Around 200-300 words is a useful starting target, not a fixed rule. Keep it shorter when there is less to say.

Use the recent interviews as the defaultSarukkai, Juneja and Murty are the closest models. The older Dutt and Mookherjee descriptions are more promotional. Keep the substance without copying generic praise or repeating the same point.

Draft in Claude

  1. Open Claude using your Decoded email. For episodes I host, open the Google Docs summary I share and paste its text into Claude. A link alone is not enough unless Claude actually reads the document.
  2. Use "Sonnet 5.5 Max" as requested: Sonnet 5.5 is the model, and Max refers to effort. Check the model and effort controls in your account. If Max is not offered, ask me rather than silently switching.
  3. Add the actual hosts, the guest's approved bio and achievements, and the correct contributor credits. Paste two recent descriptions as examples, then use the prompt below.
  4. Read the result yourself. Check every name, credential and talking point against the summary or recording. Keep any unresolved checks out of the public description.

Reusable description shape

In this episode of Decoded, [host and cohost names] sit down with [guest's full name], [relevant role and 1-3 verified achievements].

[Guest's first name] discusses [specific topic or story], including [concrete example]. We also get into [another talking point] and [the question or disagreement that makes it interesting].

The conversation ends on [the episode's closing question or takeaway].

[Confirmed contributor credits, if supplied.]

clarity before consensus.

For a solo host, use "sits down". For an event or teaching episode, adapt the opening to identify the actual speakers and format. The HERizon descriptions explain the partnership and which part of the event the listener is hearing; they do not force the standard interview opening or closing.

Upload to Spotify for Creators

  1. Use your own editor access and select Decoded. Check the episode list first so we do not upload a duplicate. These steps are Spotify's web flow for a show hosted with Spotify; if the dashboard points to another host, stop and check with me.
  2. Click New episode, then Select a file. Upload the final edited audio, not the raw recording or a previous cut. Spotify supports MP3, M4A and WAV in mono or stereo. Wait for processing.
  3. On Details, add the confirmed title and checked description. Read the Content checks and answer from the actual episode. Add season/episode numbers or episode artwork only if supplied; do not guess them.
  4. Use simple paragraphs in the description editor. Check italics, bullets and HTTPS links if used. Do not paste Markdown symbols or Claude's private fact-check notes into the public description.
  5. Listen to the beginning and end and spot-check the middle against the final file. Check the names, title, description, credits and audio duration together. If a file or fact is missing, save a draft and flag it.
  6. Follow the agreed release instruction. Now publishes immediately; Schedule uses the local time shown by Spotify. Confirm the intended date, time and timezone before scheduling. If no release instruction has been agreed, leave it as a draft and check with me.
  7. After an agreed release, open the live episode and check the title, description and playback. Send me the episode link, or the scheduled date and timezone if it is not live yet.

Before it goes live

  • Right show, right final audio, and no duplicate episode.
  • Actual hosts/cohosts named; guest achievements and talking points checked.
  • Correct credits and event context; no placeholder text, invented quotes or AI notes.
  • Title, formatting, links and release time checked. Use the supplied title; if one is needed, suggest a guest-plus-topic title rather than copying a long CV.
